The Quant Desk: replay, improve, model, advise

The Trade Performance Analyzer graduates from a scoring panel into the system's quantitative brain — five modules on one substrate, driving trade-to-trade adjustments that reduce risk and amplify every qualified opportunity.

One substrate, five lenses. Every module reads the same two immutable stores — the ledger journal (what money did) and the tick tapes (what prices did: candidate_ticks watches every candidate ~2s for 15 minutes, including tokens we refused and exits we left). Nothing is estimated that can be replayed; nothing is replayed that can't be traced to evidence. This is why the night's findings were findable at all — the tapes kept recording after our exits, which is how a 658× escape and a 1,247× never-armed run became measurable instead of invisible.

M1Replay Lab — the counterfactual machine

Deterministic tape replay: any trade, any window, any class — re-run against an alternate geometry (arm, gain-lock, trail width, rungs, clock, gates) and diff the outcome. What was hand-rolled five separate times on 07-21 (the scratch-zone replay, the trail-structure shootout, the arm sweep, the ratchet bands, the gate backtests) becomes a service with a UI.

Proven need: every major exit decision this cycle was settled by a hand-built replay — including the one that reversed my own recommendation (breakeven floors looked right and replayed −$23; the gain lock replayed +$16.50 and shipped). The lab makes that rigor the default instead of an effort.
  • Trade view: pick any position → its full tape with entry, rungs, trail floor drawn as it moved → drag the knobs → the counterfactual exit renders instantly.
  • Cohort view: select a population (class, star, venue, hour, flag) → replay a config diff across all of it → fit/holdout split enforced, plateau required — the learning loop's guardrails baked into the UI so a pretty backtest can't skip them.
  • Escape autopsy: for every labeled winner we exited early or never armed (psyopcat 658× kept $5.81; POOL 1,247× never entered), an automatic replay answers: what gate or geometry would have captured it, and what would that change cost across the whole cohort? Misses become experiments, not anecdotes.

M2Standing Quant — the nightly performance dissection

The overnight review, run by the machine every night and every hour — the exact dissection the operator specified: how signatures fired, how each wallet responded, which obvious winners existed, whether we entered, at what price, how long the hold, whether stops/TPs/trails did their jobs.

Proven need: tonight's manual pass found three never-armed 100×+ runs and an unmeasured refusal book — 18 of 50 RUG_RISK refusals ran 2×+. Facts this large should surface themselves.
  • Funnel report: fired → armed → entered → managed → banked, per signature per lane, with take-rates and the P&L of every branch not taken.
  • Refusal book: every refused candidate's full outcome tracked as a shadow position — the gates get a continuously-measured P&L of their own, so a gate that starts costing more than it saves names itself.
  • Mechanism scorecard: per exit type: activation count, capture, give-back — with drift alarms against the trailing-7-day baseline.
  • Improvement queue: each gap auto-generates a Replay Lab experiment spec; the nightly report ranks them by dollars at stake. The morning starts with a ranked worklist, not a blank page.

M3Monte Carlo Desk — modeling the money

Bootstrap resampling over the journal's real trade distributions (per class × star × venue × hour) to model what the strategy does to a bankroll — not what one history happened to do.

Proven need: the $200 top-up sizing ($4 floors, 2★ ×1.5 boost) was set by arithmetic on averages. The desk answers the real questions: probability of drawdown > X, risk of ruin at each sizing, growth-path bands $200 → $1k.
  • Equity fans: 10,000 resampled 30-day paths per proposed config → median/band outcomes, max-drawdown distribution, time-to-double estimates.
  • Sizing surface: fractional-Kelly per class/star from measured win/loss distributions — the aggression dial with confidence intervals instead of vibes.
  • Stress scenarios: rug-cluster days, venue outages, fee-regime shifts — replayed against the book to size the caps (daily loss, exposure) from tail risk rather than round numbers.

M4Live Trade Advisor — real-time, trade to trade

The Analyzer's knowledge applied while the trade is alive — the operator's "real time adjustments from trade to trade."

  • At arm: every qualified setup scored against its historical cohort — percentile EV, gap-death rate, expected hold — stamped on the position and shown on its card. A 90th-percentile setup and a 40th-percentile one stop looking identical.
  • In flight: the position's live path drawn against its class's replay envelope (p25/p50/p75 trajectories). Off-script behavior — stalling where winners accelerate, outliving the cohort's clock — surfaces as an advisory chip before the exit fires.
  • At exit: the instant grade (existing) plus the counterfactual: what the class-optimal geometry would have kept. The gap between them is M2's improvement queue, fed in real time.
  • Guardrail: the advisor informs; the genome decides. Advisory-first, exactly like the adaptive policy — promotion to acting requires the same fit/holdout evidence as any config change.

M5Venue Lab — testing the watch list without capital

Watch-list venues (Heaven, Boop, post-reset Raydium LaunchLab, quote-leg USD1 pairs) run the entire pipeline in shadow: discovered, safety-checked, routed, conviction-scored, entries and exits simulated on their real tapes — zero dollars at risk.

Proven need: the venue census caught fluxbeam's honeypot factory and Orca's zero-moon profile from data we already had. The lab makes that judgment continuous and forward-looking instead of retrospective.
  • Graduation criteria, pre-committed: n ≥ 100 shadow trades, holdout EV ≥ 1.1, rug rate within class bounds, execution route proven by on-chain simulation → auto-promote to paper. Paper proves it → operator gates live. No venue ever skips the ladder.
  • Standing census: the weekly venue table (discoveries, win/rug, best run) as a dashboard panel with trend arrows, not a hand-run query.
