The core finding

We had assumed duds and winners separate at entry. They do not — eleven candidate features were tested and all were statistically indistinguishable at the moment of entry. Separation lives in pre-peak drawdown: how deep a token dips on its way up.

Dipping is the winner signature

A 5% trailing stop mathematically survives exactly two classes — rugs and duds — and ejects every climber and every moonshot. The system was holding what it should sell and selling what it should hold. The same logic sat in the entry gate, which rejected any candidate more than 10% off its high, meaning we could only ever buy a winner at its top.

What changed

Trail rebuilt on time and new highs

a4afabe

Width is now set by token age and time since the last new high, not by drawdown depth. Young or stalled positions trail tight at 5%; established movers making new highs get 25%, and runners get 45%.

Entry gate opened to the dip

88336a2

Drawdown ceiling 10% → 40%. In the 2–3 minute window the rug rate falls as entry drawdown rises — 19.4% at 0–5% down to 0% above 20%. Held at 40 rather than the 60 discussed, because bands above 20% carry only n=10/5/3.

Entry scan 4× faster

e4242aa

Open challenge — position sizing

The multiplier chain is unstable

Paper sizes through eight compounding multipliers. Over six hours that produced positions from $0.20 to $41.64 — a 200× spread. GROKATHON was sized $0.43, rode to a 65% gain, and returned eight cents. Seven minutes later the same token was sized $5.36.

Live ignores the chain entirely, sizes off account balance in a tight $2.50–$3.86 band, and made 22× more money on the identical token. The Adaptive Policy that was designed to own this decision is still sitting in advisory mode while ad-hoc multipliers actually drive the size.

This is deliberately not being changed tonight. It is the highest-value target on the board and it deserves a designed consolidation, not a knob turned mid-session.

Late finding — the dip is the moonshot tell, and we arrive too late for it

A 784× token was reviewed that we never traded. It spent our entire 2–3 minute qualifying window down 43%, looking exactly like a dud, then recovered and ran. Measured across the corpus at the point we would actually buy, a candidate that dipped 25–45% before qualifying doubles at 52.2% versus 18.2% for one that walked up flat, and reaches 5× at 13.0% versus 2.7%.

Two caveats carry equal weight. The dipped cohort is only n=23 — far too thin to gate on, and the same sample-size trap that produced a losing size boost earlier today. And the blocker is not the drawdown ceiling we changed at 18:43: these candidates arm at 0–6.5% instantaneous drawdown, having already recovered. What excludes them is the 3-minute window closing before they come back. Any fix is a narrow, conditional window extension for dipped-and-recovering candidates — proposed for shadow-logging overnight, decided on evidence Tuesday, not shipped on 23 observations.
